Week 2: Motivation & Research

1. Project Motivation

The surge in AI technology and the growing trend of public fitness have accelerated the development of running-assistive applications. However, despite its popularity, maintaining long-term motivation remains a critical challenge, often leading to high dropout rates. Our field interviews indicate that many solo runners experience feedback poverty, where the lack of immediate reinforcement results in fatigue and disengagement.
From an Equality, Diversity, and Inclusion perspective, many existing solutions rely heavily on visual interaction, which can be inaccessible or unsafe during physical activity. This highlights the need for more inclusive, eyes-free interaction modalities. Furthermore, prior research suggests that fitness gamification often lacks a clear balance between engagement and domain appropriateness [1], limiting its long-term effectiveness.
Guided by Self-Determination Theory, this project aims to enhance users’ sense of competence and autonomy through meaningful feedback and adaptive interaction. In addition, studies show that humanized AI design can significantly improve user immersion and engagement [2]. Therefore, EchoRun explores a human-centric approach to delivering real-time, personalized, and non-visual feedback to support a more engaging and sustainable running experience..

2. Gap

Four competitor products are analysed to identify design opportunities:

Strava
▲ Strengths
  • Social Comparison Framework: Strava provides a well-established social comparison framework through features such as leaderboards, segment rankings, and friend activity feeds. These mechanisms effectively motivate users by enabling performance comparison with others, which has been shown to enhance engagement and persistence in physical activity.
  • Precision Metrics: Strava offers detailed tracking of running metrics such as pace, distance, time, and historical trends. This allows users to monitor progress over time and supports goal-oriented behavior through data-driven feedback.
  • Asynchronous Competition: Strava supports asynchronous competition by allowing users to compare their performance on predefined route segments against both their past records and other users. This enables flexible competition without requiring simultaneous participation, lowering the barrier to engagement.
▼ Weaknesses
  • Post-hoc Interaction: The app lacks real-time competitive feedback. While Strava enables performance comparison through segments and leaderboards, these comparisons are primarily post-activity. Users typically review their rankings and statistics only after completing a run, which limits the ability to influence motivation during the activity itself.
  • Visual Over-reliance: It is over-reliance on visual and post-hoc data interaction. Strava’s interaction model is heavily dependent on visual interfaces and data dashboards. During running, users have limited access to actionable feedback without actively checking their devices, where visual feedback during running may overburden the human visual perception and the need to interact with a device will divert attention from the surrounding environment[3 real time feedback] . Additionally, the platform lacks real-time, voice-based or emotionally adaptive feedback mechanisms.
  • Fragmented Awareness: It lacks limited contextual and continuous performance awareness. While Strava supports comparison through segments and historical records, its feedback is fragmented and context-dependent. Users receive insights only at specific checkpoints rather than as a continuous, evolving representation of their performance relative to others or past efforts.
Codoon
▲ Strengths
  • Professional AI Coaching: The voice guidance of the AI coach is highly professional and scientific. The coach will give customers professional suggestions such as movement correction, rhythm adjustment and exercise intensity control by combining real-time sports data.
  • Long-term Memory: The AI coach has a long-term memory function, which can record all-dimensional user data such as body’s feedback, habit and preference. It continuously optimizes the personalization of the voice feedback based on data accumulation, evolving from generalized suggestions to exclusive guidance tailored to the users personal sports ability.
  • Pace Rabbit Feature: The app is equipped with a dedicated Pace Rabbit AI feature, where the function creates customized pace training plans for runners and provides a pace stability score to enhance pace control and ultimately helps customers achieve personal bests in marathon and other running events.
▼ Weaknesses
  • Static Interaction: The AI coach lacks competitiveness and adaptability to competitive scenarios, which stands in sharp contrast to the core design of our project that centers on constructing a competitive voice interaction logic which is empowering runners to compare their real-time running data with their own historical records or friends’ shared data. Recent research has evidenced that the presence of a competitor can significantly boost physical effort across both short and long exercise duration[4,fronties]. Moreover, competitiveness has been proven to enhance physical motivation, including the intrinsic motivation to persist in sports practice[4].
  • Rigid AI Personality: There is no differentiated AI personality setting. The voice style is fixed as a unified professional tone with no optional styles customized via prompts that leads to a rigid interactive experience that struggles to arouse runners’ emotional engagement during exercise.
  • Limited Trigger Logic: The voice trigger logic is single and lacks dynamic design. It cannot provide accurate voice feedback for the real-time changes in the distance difference between runners and target data, and is unable to adapt to competitive scenarios such as Ghost Run that require real-time tracking of data differences.
Yodo Run
▲ Strengths
  • Diversified Incentives: The app has diversified reward mechanisms with dual cash and virtual incentives. It has a multi-layered reward system combining platform-initiated benefits and user competition benefits. Users can get instant cash reward by completing daily sports goals, and also participate in various sports events to obtain virtual medals and honor certificates.
  • Growth Hierarchy: It has a hierarchical medal and level growth system. Users can unlock exclusive medals and honor logos by completing specific challenges and gain experience points to upgrade. The upgraded level can be exchanged for advanced sports courses and exclusive virtual image decorations, and the real-time updated friend ranking list further stimulates users' competitive awareness and sports stickiness.
  • Motion Analysis: Equipped with self-developed AI skeleton recognition and motion analysis algorithms, it can capture motion postures in real time, correct wrong movements through voice feedback, and customize personalized weekly or monthly training plans according to users' physical fitness levels and sports goals.
▼ Weaknesses
  • Intrinsic Motivation Undermining: Extant psychological research provides compelling evidence that tangible rewards exert a detrimental undermining effect on individuals’ intrinsic motivation for simple tasks[5,APA]. The agreed running requires users to pay a deposit to join the group, and the completed users divide the deposit of the uncompleted ones. This money-related reward and punishment mechanism not only brings psychological moral binding to users, but also easily triggers vicious competition among users, making sports deviate from the original health purpose and become a utilitarian behavior for chasing money interests.
  • Data Sync Errors: The platform’s sports data statistics for reward activities has obvious synchronization errors, leading to unfair judgment of activity completion. Users may fail to obtain corresponding rewards due to non-subjective data errors, which seriously reduces the credibility of the reward mechanism.
  • Accessibility Barriers: Although the platform sets up daily sports red envelope rewards, the cash withdrawal has strict restrictions , and there are frequent problems such as delayed withdrawal and malicious deduction of small-amount rewards. The low actual accessibility of cash rewards makes the incentive effect of the reward mechanism greatly discounted.
Walkr
▲ Strengths
  • Narrative Gamification: It provides highly engaging gamified experience. Walkr transforms physical activity into a game-like experience by converting steps into in-game resources, enabling users to explore virtual planets and progress within a narrative system. This strong gamification design enhances user engagement and enjoyment.
  • Low Entry Barrier: The barrier is low to entry and access. Walkr requires minimal effort from users, as it passively tracks daily steps without demanding structured exercise routines. This lowers the entry barrier for beginners and casual users, making it inclusive for a broader population.
  • Instant Reinforcement: The app provides frequent in-game rewards and a clear sense of progression, which offers immediate positive reinforcement. This helps address the delayed gratification problem commonly found in fitness activities.
▼ Weaknesses
  • Non-Exercise Focus: Walkr is lack of exercise-specific feedback and training value. While Walkr effectively promotes physical activity, it does not provide detailed or actionable feedback related to exercise performance. A research has claimed that an automated method with mobile phone sensors and computational ability do well in help people to maintain a longer motivation in exercising[6,the mobile] As a result, it offers limited support for users seeking structured training or performance improvement.
  • Weak Contextual Linkage: The integration of physical activity and game mechanics is weak. The connection between physical activity and in-game outcomes in Walkr is largely indirect, as step counts are simply converted into abstract resources. This lack of contextual linkage reduces the meaningfulness of the activity itself and may weaken long-term engagement for users seeking more purpose-driven interaction.
  • Data Simplicity: The data Walkr primarily relies on basic step-count data collected from the mobile device, with limited integration of richer fitness metrics such as pace, distance, or real-time performance indicators. This restricts the system’s ability to provide meaningful, context-aware feedback and reduces its applicability for more structured or performance-oriented physical activities.

Core Pain Point

The "Silent Drop": When Brian falls behind, there is zero feedback unless he glances at his screen. Like the "Black Box Wait" scenario in lecture, the user feels a total loss of control — and unlike that case, looking down mid-sprint is physically dangerous.

Design Opportunity

Proactive Audio Transparency: The event-triggered AI coach fires a contextual audio cue the moment the distance gap crosses a threshold. This converts the frustration dip into a motivational spike — directly addressing the deepest drop-off on the emotional curve.

2. Playful Requirements List

Ghost Mode: Racing Against Yourself & Friends

Why it's fun

Running alone is often a chore. This feature turns a workout into a "Mario Kart" style time-trial, tapping into our competitive nature to win a real-time race.

Implementation

Overlay a "Ghost" runner on the live map based on historical GPS data, allowing users to track their relative position and pace.

AI Buddy: Coaching with Personality

Why it's fun

No more cold, robotic data. Our AI Coach acts like a "buddy" who cheers or playfully roasts you, creating a sense of companionship and reducing mental fatigue.

Implementation

Trigger personality-driven feedback (voice/text) based on real-time performance milestones or pace drops.

Points & Rank: Leveling Up Your Run

Why it's fun

By giving every step a tangible "score," the fitness journey feels more like an RPG progression system, satisfying the human need for status and achievement.

Implementation

A point-based economy where users earn rewards for beating Ghost records, used to unlock badges and climb global leaderboards.

Option A — Continuous Screen Display

Always-on visual dashboard (Strava-style)

✔ Familiar to experienced runners
✔ Rich data visibility

✘ Requires repeated screen glances
✘ Safety hazard on roads
✘ Breaks Flow State

Rejected — contradicts our eyes-free principle and increases cognitive load, the exact problem we aim to solve.

Option B — Real-time AI Agent

Live LLM call on every trigger event

✔ Fully dynamic responses
✔ More conversational feel

✘ API latency 1 to 3 seconds destroys the emotional moment
✘ Requires constant network
✘ Cost unsustainable at free tier

Rejected — high fidelity that limits our ability to rapidly pivot if the concept fails, exactly the trade-off warned against in the lecture.

✓ Chosen

Option C — Glanceable UI + Event-Triggered Local Audio

Local rule engine + pre-rendered persona TTS

✔ Zero-latency audio under 200 ms
✔ Works fully offline
✔ WoZ-testable at 70% fidelity
✔ Matches prototype fidelity to the question we need to answer now

✘ Pre-scripted responses less varied
✘ Threshold calibration needed

Chosen — best supports runner Flow State, enables safe eyes-free use, and lets us validate our core hypothesis via a WoZ study before committing to full AI integration.

Week 6–10: Technical Implementation

1. System Architecture

a. System Architecture

Our system is designed on a three-tier architecture: a Vue-based Mobile Frontend, a Spring Boot Backend, and external AI Cloud Services.

  • Frontend Logic: The Local Rule Engine calculates the gap (△ D) between the user and the "Ghost" runner locally to ensure zero-latency interaction.
  • Intelligence Layer: Real-time performance data triggers the LLM & TTS pipeline, transforming raw metrics into personality-driven audio coaching.
  • Data Persistence: All trajectories are stored as JSON objects in MySQL, allowing for asynchronous competition with past selves or friends.

(2) PERFORMANCE RESULTS
  • Task completion rate: 3/3 users successfully triggered Ghost Mode.
  • Average setup time: Approximately 45 seconds from entering the page to starting a ghost run.
  • First-time confusion: One user did not realize they needed to follow the sequence of "select a ghost record first, then click Challenge" to start Ghost Mode, and spent time looking for an independent "Ghost Run Mode" entrance. In reality, the system has no separate entrance—selecting a record and clicking Challenge automatically switches to Ghost Mode.
(3) KEY OBSERVATIONS
  • Users had difficulty and confusion when selecting a ghost and starting the ghost run. They could not quickly find the Ghost Run entrance. We should make the ghost run selection and challenge section clearer.
  • Users were confused about the running interface's track display. Our current track is a fake spiral graphic (hardcoded), not the user's real running route, making users think our running GPS might have issues.

3. Final Reflection

Social & Ethical Implications

The core design concept of EchoRun is "Positive Pressure". Through AI ghost running companions and stylized voice coaching, we aim to lower the psychological barrier to starting running, making exercise more fun and accompanied. In the current social context, sedentary lifestyles and lack of exercise have become widespread health issues. EchoRun attempts to use a low-cost digital companionship solution to motivate more people to go outside and develop regular exercise habits.

However, this design also brings ethical issues that need reflection. First, although the "sarcastic coach" increases engagement and fun, it is not suitable for everyone. For some users (such as exercise beginners or those with low self-efficacy), continuous sarcastic feedback may have the opposite effect, even increasing anxiety. To address this, we provide a gentle, encouraging voice option in the system, allowing users to choose their preferred companionship style based on their own mental state, reflecting respect for user psychological diversity.

Second, the "ghost running" mechanism in EchoRun simulates the experience of competing with others. This design satisfies users' social and competitive needs to some extent, but may also lead to excessive competition or data comparison anxiety. Our solution is: the ghost's data comes from the user's own past records, not from other runners' data, thus transforming competition into "comparing with your past self," weakening the pressure of comparing with others and emphasizing self-growth.

From a broader social impact perspective, AI sports companionship tools like EchoRun could be applied in community health promotion and school physical education in the future, providing a low-threshold, personalized motivational method for those lacking sports community support. At the same time, we recognize that digital tools cannot completely replace real interpersonal companionship and a sense of community belonging. Therefore, EchoRun positions itself as an "assistant" rather than a "substitute."

The Use of AI in This Project (P-O-A Format)

Case 1 UI Style Iteration: From "Geek Dark" to "Sunny Bright"
P (Prompt) "Help me write a cool running data card component. It needs to display various data needed by runners, and make the data display dynamic and prominent so users can get all core information at a glance."
O (Outcome) AI generated a tech-heavy component with a dark background and neon accents. The overall style was very geeky and cool, but it did not match EchoRun's desired identity of being sunny, bright, warm, and encouraging outdoor running. Moreover, because each data display component was too large, the interface became cluttered and users didn't know where to look. There were also layout issues with component misalignment.
A (Action) We kept the card framework generated by AI (rounded corners, shadows, information hierarchy), but completely rewrote the color scheme: changed the dark background to white, and changed neon colors to more normal colors, transforming the interface from a "geeky dark room" to a "sunny playground" visual feel. We also manually adjusted component sizes and layouts to make the interface more aesthetically pleasing.
Case 2 Mock Data Diversity: Covering Runners of Different Levels
P (Prompt) "Generate a set of running history records in JSON format, conforming to the React frontend storage.js format, containing core fields: id, runnerName (Mika), title (Morning Jog), distance (2000 meters), duration (600 seconds), date (ISO format). Store one point per second in the format { "t": seconds, "d": cumulative distance (meters) at that second }."
O (Outcome) AI provided structurally correct data, but the data was too artificial. All records were concentrated around 5–10 kilometers with a pace of 5'30"-6'00", almost identical, with no pace variation throughout the run. Moreover, due to the large volume of data, long sequences often had errors (missing data in the middle), and it did not cover scenarios for beginners (short distance, slow pace) or advanced runners (long distance, fast pace).
A (Action) We reduced the total distance of the records, requesting generation of short-distance records below 500 meters to facilitate testing while reducing the error rate of AI-generated data. We also introduced segmented description instructions (e.g., "first 60s for warm-up, next 60s for sprint") to guide AI to generate more realistic data for a more authentic user experience during usability testing.

AI Takeaways: AI did improve development efficiency and filled some gaps in our front-end knowledge. Many times, we had an idea, and AI provided us with technical options to implement it, greatly reducing our learning cost. However, it also has many problems. For example, AI cannot make design trade-offs; we still need to constantly innovate to balance technical issues with practical outcomes. Moreover, AI-generated code often has issues where it "seems correct but actually has problems" — it runs without errors, but problems are discovered during testing. A simple example: we had a component to turn off the AI coach's voice, but after turning it off, it couldn't be turned back on. These types of minor errors were very common in the code, requiring significant effort to detect and fix. Therefore, AI-generated code still requires our own review and testing. Also, the longer the chain of instructions given to AI, the higher the probability of errors. This forces us to break down the entire task into small, clear subtasks to ensure accuracy and effectiveness. But breaking it down introduces another AI problem—memory. The UI components generated from two separate tasks can end up with completely different styles. Although AI provided huge assistance in code generation, the core soul of EchoRun — the gameplay logic of "ghost racing," the closed-loop design of user experience, and the final usability evaluation — were all completed by our team ourselves.
